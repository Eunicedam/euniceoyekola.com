Why your Meta ads get clicks but no sales

People are clicking but nobody is buying. Work through these five checks before you spend another penny on a bigger budget.

You check your ads and the numbers look busy. People are clicking. Your store is getting visitors. But the orders aren't coming. It's one of the most common problems I see, and the good news is that it's usually not your product. It's something between the click and the checkout.

Before you raise your budget, work through these checks in order. More budget on a broken funnel only means losing money faster.

1. Check that your Pixel is tracking purchases

If Meta can't see who buys, it can't find more buyers. It will find more clickers instead, because clicks are the only signal it gets.

  • Open Events Manager and make sure your Purchase event fires when an order is completed, and only then.
  • Check that the order value is being sent, so Meta can tell a big order from a small one.
  • Set up the Conversions API alongside the Pixel. Browser tracking alone misses some sales.

If your campaign is optimised for clicks or traffic, switch it to purchases once tracking works. Meta gives you exactly what you ask for.

2. Make sure the product page matches the ad

Someone clicks an ad showing a bundle at a special price, then lands on a page with a different photo, a different price and no mention of the offer. Most of them leave in seconds.

  • Send people to the exact product or offer they saw, not your homepage.
  • Use the same images, the same price and the same words as the ad.
  • Put the main benefit and the price where they can be seen without scrolling.

3. Open your store on your phone

Most people clicking your ads are on a phone. So test your store the way they see it.

  • Does the page load quickly on mobile data?
  • Can you see the "Add to cart" button without hunting for it?
  • Are shipping costs and delivery times clear before checkout, not a surprise at the end?

4. Give people a reason to trust you

New visitors don't know your brand yet. Reviews, clear return and refund policies, real contact details and secure payment badges all reduce the doubt that stops a first order.

5. Look at the offer itself

Sometimes everything works and the offer just isn't strong enough to make a stranger buy today. Bundles, a small first-order discount, free shipping over a set amount or a gift with purchase can all give people a reason to act now rather than "later".

What to do next

Fix tracking first, because every other decision depends on good data. Then fix the page, then the offer. Only when those are working is it time to add budget.
